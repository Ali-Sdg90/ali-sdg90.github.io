I started learning JavaScript in 2022, as I continued through the CS Internship program.

At that point, I was still getting comfortable with HTML and CSS when suddenly a completely different language showed up. A language that worked nothing like the HTML and CSS I had been learning.

I learned it on my way to university, through YouTube videos, the You Don't Know JS books, and most importantly, W3Schools. I think around 90% of what I learned back then came from W3Schools!

Learning it was both hard and easy. I already had experience with C++ and understood programming logic, but JavaScript was still different. I remember writing "JS" on my thumb whenever I went to university, just to remind myself that learning this language was my main goal at the time. I wanted to keep it on my mind and regularly review whatever I had learned.

I also had a 60-page notebook that I turned into my own little JavaScript dictionary. Whenever I learned something new, I would write it down neatly and make it look nice. I kept going back through that notebook so I could remember everything I had learned before.

My learning was project-based from the very beginning. Like pretty much everything else I've learned, I had a repository called Learning JS. I would take even the simplest concepts, write some code, see what happened, change things around, and play with them until I really understood how they worked.

After a while, I built my first proper JavaScript project, a Todo App. I made the whole thing in about three or four hours, recorded a nice timelapse of the process, and uploaded it to YouTube.

Building things with JavaScript was really fun. I could finally do more than just change how a page looked. I could change elements, give them behavior, and kind of give them a personality. That was cool!

Then came a much bigger challenge: building a calculator. I didn't want to make something that simply worked. I wanted it to handle complicated calculations too, with multiple expressions, automatic parentheses, calculation history, memory, and all sorts of other features. The logic behind that calculator ended up being pretty complicated, and the result is still something I'm proud of.

After that, I kept coming up with new projects to practice JavaScript and get more comfortable with it. From XO, which is still one of my favorite projects, to Spread Color, Quick Math, Sticky Scroll, Aim Lab, and plenty of other experiments.

That was pretty much how I liked learning things. Whenever I came across a new concept, I'd start thinking about what I could build with it. Preferably a game, a simulation, a sandbox, or anything I could play around with and enjoy making. Then I'd build it and have fun with it!

I was never really interested in building boring e-commerce websites. They just weren't exciting to me, and I didn't get that feeling of creating something fun or interesting. So I kept choosing projects that I actually wanted to make.

Later, when I became a technical mentor at CS Internship, I started helping other interns learn JavaScript too. I guided quite a few of them, held classes for some, and even gave them exercises and assignments to work on.

JavaScript is one of the main foundations of my technical world. I like its logic, I'm comfortable with it, and I think it's an incredibly powerful language. Over the following years, Node.js, React, Expo, React Native, and many other technologies were built on top of it.

And to think it all goes back to those days when I was writing "JS" on my thumb at university, carrying around my little notebook, and trying to get to know this language better.

Now JavaScript isn't really a friend to me. It's a language I speak. It's become a part of me and the way I express myself.

I love it. :)
