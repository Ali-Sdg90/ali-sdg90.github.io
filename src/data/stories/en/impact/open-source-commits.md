I've always been interested in volunteering and doing things simply because I enjoy them and choose to do them. Working on open-source projects is one of the things that feels closely connected to that interest.

I've been pretty active in open-source projects, and most of that work and my commits were related to CS Internship. The program itself was open source, and many of the projects and tools we built for it were hosted in its GitHub Organization.

Part of my work involved creating and improving courses and educational materials for different parts of the program. Another part was developing tools and bots that were actually used inside the program, like CS Club Bot, CS Queue Bot, CS Queue Calendar, and other tools we built to make things easier.

One of the things I really cared about while building these projects was the quality and structure of the code. I tried to follow good software engineering practices as much as possible, from setting up CI/CD pipelines and writing tests with high coverage to keeping the code organized and maintainable.

The reason was that these projects weren't supposed to stay in my hands forever. I was contributing these tools to the program, and potentially dozens of other people would work on them in the future, change things, or add new features. I wasn't always going to be there to look after them, so it mattered to me that what I built had a good structure and was easy for others to work with.

Outside of CS Internship, I also contributed to other open-source projects like fx-blox, TonRich, and a few smaller ones.

I've always enjoyed teamwork and open source. I like the idea of people with different experiences and ideas working on the same project, each contributing their own part to move it forward. I've had some really good experiences with this kind of collaboration, and I'd like to do more of it in the future.
