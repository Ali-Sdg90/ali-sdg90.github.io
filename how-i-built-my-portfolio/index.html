  1. English

    First Vision

    I'd always wanted a personal portfolio: one place to show everything I'd built, but not just another plain page. I wanted it to feel completely like Ali, something that made it obvious from the start that it came from my own creativity and personality.

    Something like a hub: a central place for my projects, resume, social links, and everything else connected to me.

    When life got a little quieter and I felt ready to begin, I had the idea of building a bookshelf where each book represented one of my projects. A simple, cozy bookshelf.

    I even thought it could be fun to have a stop-motion version of me standing beside it, waving, following the cursor with his eyes, with a few small easter eggs hidden around the page.

    I sketched the first version of the idea in Paint, and that became the project's first rough design.

    Two Different Worlds

    For testing, I took a photo of myself wearing the same outfit and using the same lighting angle as the design, then placed it in the project.

    It was honestly disappointing to realize that my photo didn't work well on the page.

    The lighting angle, image quality, and overall realism all needed to be perfect. If even one part felt slightly off, the image stopped looking natural and made the whole page less comfortable to look at. I realized that continuing with this design would put the entire project at a high risk of failure.

    There was another problem too. My photo drew too much attention. The shelf was supposed to be the focal point, and having two parts of the page constantly competing for attention wasn't good for the experience.

    So it was a failed but useful experiment, and I knew I needed to change the design.
